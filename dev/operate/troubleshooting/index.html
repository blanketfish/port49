    What each device does when a part of port49 is down Failure matrix

    Three checks answer most first questions. curl <port49d-host>:9049/readyz says whether port49d is serving: after a failing store closes its listener it answers 503 waiting for the store. port49 store status (on a host that reaches the metrics port) prints whether each store answers, and exits 3 when one does not. port49 explain --device <device> asks the broker, through your agent, what port49 would do for you on that device.

    A person tells you…¶

    "I cannot log in to one device"¶

    You see: one person, or everyone, fails on one device; the same logins work on other devices.

    It means: the fault is in what port49 knows about that device or that person's grant on it. The decision log record for the attempt carries a reason:

    • no_grants_matched: no grant of theirs selects the device.
    • token_not_found or token_spent: the token expired, was already used, or was minted for another device (tokens, receipts and contexts).
    • An unvalidated record, and a connection the device sees reset with no reply: no key validated what the device sent. unvalidated.diagnosis says why: no_known_key_matches (a wrong key), matches_retired, unadmitted_source (the address is not in the inventory, which unvalidated.source names; a device whose source address changed looks exactly like this), device_quarantined, admission_refused or transport_mismatch (the device reached the listener it does not speak).

    Do this: have the person run port49 explain --device <device>; it names the grant and the reason. Look the attempt up in the decision log by device.nas, or by source address for an unvalidated record. For a key fault, give the device the current key (port49 keys show).

    Don't: restart port49d or the broker, which does not cure a fault confined to one device. Don't widen the policy to "see if it works".

    "I cannot log in to any device"¶

    You see: logins fail everywhere, or devices ask for a local password. Alerts that may be firing: port49_target_down, port49_target_absent, port49_store_health_failures_close.

    It means: one of four things:

    • port49d is down or unreachable. The devices find TCP 49 refused and fall to their local method.
    • port49d is up and its store is failing. It answers a token login ERROR store_unavailable and every command FAIL store_unavailable, then closes its listeners after 5.5 seconds at the defaults, and devices fall to local.
    • The broker is down. No new token can be minted; a token already held still logs in, and an open session runs on.
    • port49d cannot read meta/witness after a restore (port49_redemption_restore_unjudged). It refuses every token login until a read answers.

    Do this: curl <port49d-host>:9049/readyz and port49 store status; read the first alert that fired; then the matching row of a cut region at 3am or the failure matrix. Use break-glass while you fix it.

    Don't: restart port49d in a loop to "wake it": while the store is down it waits with its listener closed and comes back by itself. Don't change the devices' AAA configuration; they already fall to local.

    "Device break-glass does not work"¶

    You see: a local account is refused on a device while port49 is failing. This is the device's own local login, not the offline revoke in Revoking access.

    It means: it depends on the platform, and on how long the device has been failing.

    • An ERROR is final on some platforms. IOS XE, Junos under authentication-order tacplus alone, and XR over TLS refuse the local account while port49d answers ERROR (its store is failing). Local works once port49d closes its listener (about 5.5 seconds at the defaults) or is stopped. What each platform does is in the failure matrix and on its device page.
    • A device that failed over keeps answering locally for a while. The device itself answers for a window and sends port49 nothing: a token login is refused during it, and break-glass works. Measured: IOS XE 30 to 39 seconds over TCP (note), about a minute over TLS on one release, measured once, and IOS XR 24 to 46 seconds (note). XR also accepts any local account's password in that window, including one port49 denies. Expect the window after port49d returns from a stop. The cells are in deploy/device-aaa/README.md.

    Unmeasured

    Whether an authentication ERROR is final on Cisco IOS XR over plain TCP is not measured (L93, open). Plan as if break-glass is refused until port49d closes its listener, and keep a console path.

    Do this: to get to break-glass at once, stop port49d: the devices then find TCP 49 refused and use their local method. Otherwise wait for the listener to close, or for the device's window to end. Keep a console or second path for IOS XE and XR, and test break-glass on each platform before you need it.

    Don't: try to shorten a device's window with deadtime on IOS XE: it does not accept the command. Don't leave local accounts on XR beyond break-glass ones.

    "My session ended early"¶

    You see: a person's session ended before they expected: port49 ssh exits 255, the agent says the session ended, or a command is denied context_stale or credential_revoked.

    It means: one of the planned ends. The refresh or revoke record just before the denial names it:

    • the session reached max_session_age, or a token ran past receipt_idle_ttl or receipt_max_age (token_spent);
    • an administrator revoked the person (revocation);
    • an OIDC back-channel logout ended it;
    • a policy change withdrew their grant, or the directory no longer maps them;
    • context_stale: no directory refresh verified them for session.staleness_ceiling (4 hours by default). port49_directory_refresh_half_ceiling pages at half of that.

    Do this: read that person's last records in the decision log. For a stale context, restore the directory servers; the person signs in again once a refresh passes.

    Don't: lift a revocation before reading the revoke record, which says who made it and why. Don't set session.staleness_ceiling to 0s to quiet context_stale: that removes the bound on how old a verification may be.

    "It says the principal was revoked at …"¶

    You see: port49 token or port49 ssh prints this principal was revoked at <time>; nothing is minted for it until an operator lifts the revocation (port49 revoke --lift). A certificate revoked at its CA reads this certificate was revoked at its CA (<origin>) and marked at <time>; nothing is minted for it; request a new certificate.

    It means: a standing revocation mark is in the store, and the person is refused credential_revoked at every mint. The mark does not expire.

    Do this: port49 revoke --list shows the marks in force. If the revocation was a mistake, lift it: port49 revoke --principal <principal> --lift. A certificate the CA revoked is not fixed by a lift: the person needs a new certificate.

    Don't: undo a revocation by restoring the store from a snapshot: a restore is the one routine operation that can admit a revoked person. Don't lift a mark you did not read first.

    An admin command is refused¶

    You see: port49 revoke, port49 directory ... or another admin command fails with a status and a reason.

    It means: the broker refused the call. A refusal before any key press means your subject lacks the right in the policy's api: list (revoke_others, lift_revocations): see Admin access.

    The other refusals (the key's attestation does not verify, no attestation roots, no touch in time) have a different fix for each deployment. The fix is on the troubleshoot page for systemd, containers or Helm. With every broker down, use the offline revoke in Revoking access.

    Don't: retry a refused touch in a loop: the alert port49_admin_touch_refused pages on a run of them.

    "port49 ssh sends an empty password"¶

    You see: port49 ssh connects and the device prompts for or accepts nothing: the login fails as though the password were empty.

    It means: ssh could not run port49 as its askpass helper, because the binary is setgid or has file capabilities. Install it with mode 0755, owned by root: Install the client.

    Do this: check ls -l $(command -v port49) for mode -rwxr-xr-x and no setgid bit, and getcap $(command -v port49) for no output.

    Don't: work around it with sshpass or a password in a script.

    A log line says…¶

    Each You see is the line as port49 writes it, with <…> for the part that varies.

    Cached directory membership¶

    You see: serving <principal>'s cached directory membership (its pin as this region last read it)

    It means: the whole line begins directory store: not answering: and ends a forget made elsewhere reaches this region when the directory answers. This region cannot reach the directory store, and port49 answered for that principal from the pin it last read. A forget made in another region has not arrived here. It is said once per principal per 10 minutes, so on a fleet past 65,536 principals it can repeat.

    Do this: restore the link to the directory store (port49 store status; the alert port49_store_directory_unreachable). When it heals, the forget reaches the region by itself (stores).

    Don't: run port49 directory forget in the cut region: it is refused there. Run it from a region that reaches the directory. Don't size the outage by counting these lines.

    The logout queue is full¶

    You see: the logout queue is full; a pass over the whole logout set follows

    It means: the broker's runner received more back-channel logouts from another region than its queue holds. None is lost: a pass over the whole set reaches them. port49_broker_logouts_queue_full_total counts each logout that found the queue full.

    Do this: nothing at once. If the counter keeps rising, the runner is behind: look for a store alert.

    Don't: restart the broker to empty the queue; the pass is what delivers the logouts.

    A logout did not land¶

    You see: a logout token from <issuer> could not be applied: the store did not answer; answered 503 so the provider may retry

    It means: an identity provider's back-channel logout reached the broker while its store was down. The broker answered 503 so the provider may retry. The line ends with the commands to use if it does not, in one of two forms. The provider's event log shows the 503.

    Do this: both forms run through your agent and need the revoke_others and lift_revocations rights (Revoking access). Form 1 means the logout wrote its record and a later step failed: if the decision log has a revoke record with origin backchannel_logout for the principal just before the line, end its contexts with port49 revoke --principal <principal>, then lift it with port49 revoke --lift --principal <principal>, and last check that port49 revoke --list shows no mark for it. Form 2 means nothing was written, so no logout record refuses the held ID token or issued certificate: revoke the principal the same way, and lift it only after the time the line prints, when that token or certificate has expired. For a token that names only a sid, the line first offers port49 revoke --sid <sid> --sid-issuer <issuer>, which ends that one session and leaves no mark to lift.

    Don't: lift a Form 2 revocation early: the held token can mint again.

    The session store was restored from a snapshot¶

    You see: the session store was restored from a snapshot

    It means: port49d read meta/witness and found that the store went back to an earlier moment. Tokens minted before the restore are refused token_spent once and the users mint again. Revocations and marks made after the snapshot are lost. The full line names the floor revision and says to follow the restore runbook.

    Do this: follow backup and restore, from the revocations step. Users run port49 token again.

    Don't: restart port49d to clear the line, or lift anything, before the runbook says. Don't assume a revoked person is still revoked until the revocations are re-issued.

    Other signs of a restore, with what to do:

    • port49_store_restore_detected_timestamp_seconds above 0 and the alert port49_store_restore_detected: the same event as a metric. Do the same.
    • A login fails and port49_redemption_restored_refused_total rises: the token is from before the restore. The user mints a new one (port49 token).
    • A broker says runner: <why>; every unspent token is purged and must be minted again (port49 token): the runner purged. Nothing to do; every user mints again.
    • A broker says runner: N revocation(s) in broker.state_dir/marks.json were missing from the store ... and are put back: lift again, with port49 revoke --lift, any that were lifted on purpose before the snapshot.
    • A broker says broker.state_dir had no marks.json at this start: it put back nothing. If no broker's state_dir survived, re-issue every revocation made since the snapshot (step 4 of the runbook).
    • port49_broker_mint_gate_closed at 1 and POST /v1/token answering 503 store_unavailable with Retry-After: 5: a broker owes the purge and mints nothing until it is done. Wait. If it stays at 1 for more than a minute (ticket port49_broker_mint_gate_closed), check that one broker holds the keyspace and read its stderr for a purge that failed. The next takeover makes a cut-short purge again.
    • port49_redemption_restore_unjudged at 1 and port49d: store: reading meta/witness, to tell whether the store was restored: ...; token redemptions are refused (store_unavailable): port49d cannot read meta/witness, so it refuses every token login (page after 2 minutes: port49_redemption_restore_unjudged). Fix the store, or the daemon role's read of meta/witness. A device whose authentication ERROR is final (IOS XE, and Junos under tacplus alone) refuses break-glass until the read answers or port49d is stopped. Do not delete restore.json to clear it.

    A token was minted ahead of this host's clock¶

    You see: clock: a token was minted <N> ahead of this host's clock, at most once every ten minutes, and port49_token_minted_ahead_total rising; the ticket port49_token_clock_skew fires on it.

    It means: the broker that minted the token has a clock N ahead of this port49d's, so every token from that broker lives N longer here. The other direction does not show: a broker N behind shortens each token, and past a token_ttl its tokens are dead on arrival, so every login with one fails token_not_found with no other sign. If logins from one broker fail token_not_found right after the token was minted, compare the clocks first (Requirements).

    Do this: put every broker host and every port49d host on NTP (chronyc tracking, or timedatectl) and confirm they agree to well under the smallest token_ttl. Alert on the hosts' own NTP sync, since port49 cannot see a broker that is behind (Requirements).

    Don't: lengthen token_ttl to cover the skew: it widens replay for an unspent token and leaves the clocks wrong for everything else.

    Shedding¶

    You see: shedding: all <N> TACACS+ connection slots (limits.max_connections) are in use

    It means: port49d's global connection cap is full and each new connection is reset at accept, so the device tries its next server. It is said when shedding begins, then once a minute as still shedding: <N> connections reset at Accept in the last minute; <N> of <N> slots in use.

    Do this: raise limits.max_connections or add replicas (monitoring). The alerts are under Connections.

    Don't: treat it as an outage: devices are being sent to their next server on purpose.

    A directory pin is not re-sealed or does not open¶

    You see: directory store: <principal>'s pin does not open and the alert port49_directory_pins_unopenable, on the keyspace holder (port49_store_keyspace_held 1).

    It means: the broker's scheduled pass could not open a pin, which is a pinned principal's directory entry sealed under the encryption: key. For an unknown key (ErrUnknownKey), a key was retired while it still sealed the pin. Bind, mint and refresh fail for that principal. From the code (not measured), sessions already open are denied context_stale once session.staleness_ceiling has passed. Each pass opens the pin again, so port49_crypt_open_failures_total{record="membership"} rises at least once per pass for each such pin. The pass never deletes or rewrites it, and says the line once an hour for at most 20 principals a pass (the first 20 in sorted order); the count's line says how many more are not named.

    Other lines of the same pass, each once an hour, with no gauge change:

    • directory store: the re-seal sealed N pins under the current key; M are left under encryption.previous: expected during step 2 of a rotation. No action.
    • directory store: the re-seal of pins stopped: <error>; the next pass starts again: the directory store stopped answering or refused the broker's identity.
    • directory store: <principal>'s pin could not be written under the current key (...): that pin is counted in port49_directory_pins_sealed_previous, and the pass goes on.
    • directory store: the re-seal of pins ran out of its <budget> budget after sealing N pins (M did not open); the next pass goes on from there.

    Do this: for an unknown key, put the retired key back as previous and restart, which deletes nothing and lets the next pass re-seal the pin. If the line says the record does not match its seal, it was changed outside port49 and no key helps: run port49 directory forget <principal>. If the key is gone, forget also frees the principal, and the person's next sign-in pins them again. Any admin with forget_principals whose own pin opens can run it. Confirm that the person who signs in is the same person (Stores). There is no offline forget on port49-broker.

    Don't: remove encryption.previous while port49_directory_pins_sealed_previous is above 0 (Key rotation).

    A directory outage past 24 hours¶

    You see: no_grants_matched (or identity_unmapped) for people who held sessions, right after a broker restart or a policy reload, during or just after a long directory outage.

    It means: a person's directory membership is stored with a lease of 24 hours from its last verification (broker.directory.grace cannot exceed 24 hours). During an outage nothing refreshes it. Past the lease, a broker restart or any policy reload finds no membership and revokes every directory session, whatever session.staleness_ceiling is, including 0s. It fails closed. Before 24 hours, sessions continue until the ceiling.

    Do this: bring the directory back before 24 hours. When it is back, the people sign in again.

    Don't: restart a broker or edit the policy during a directory outage that is approaching 24 hours. Raising staleness_ceiling does not help.

    Revocations file failed¶

    You see: port49_revocations_file_failed at 1.

    It means: the last write of broker.revocations_file failed (a hung or full disk or mount). The revocation itself stands in the store and in force, but the file is behind, so a restart would forget a mark the file does not hold. port49 revoke exits 3 for this.

    Do this: fix the disk or mount the file lives on, then run the revoke again. Only a revoke or a lift writes the file, so after a write error nothing retries on its own and the gauge stays at 1 until you do. The revoke rewrites the file with every mark in force, and the gauge falls to 0. After a hung mount returns, the write in flight finishes the catch-up itself, but run the revoke anyway if the gauge has not fallen.

    Don't: restart the broker or port49d while it is at 1.
