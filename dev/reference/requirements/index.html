    Requirements¶

    Every deployment needs the five things below. The install pages for systemd, containers and Helm each have a step that meets them.

    Device source address¶

    port49d admits a device by the address it sees, so nothing in front of port 49 may rewrite the device's source address. A NAT or a proxy in front of port 49 breaks admission.

    • On a host, this is the default.
    • In a container, a published port can change the address. See Install with containers.
    • In Kubernetes, a LoadBalancer Service needs externalTrafficPolicy: Local, which is the chart's default. Cluster makes port49d see a pod-network or node address. The measurements are on Install with Helm.

    etcd¶

    port49 runs on its own etcd cluster of three members, never another system's. It needs port49's two roles (port49d store-roles prints them), compaction, a quota and backups.

    A member must be etcd 3.4.24, 3.5.8 or 3.6 or later. port49d refuses an older one and says which. Its start line names every member it checked, for example etcd https://etcd-1.example.com:2379 3.6.15, ... at or above the floor (3.4.24, 3.5.8 or 3.6). Every config names every member's URL, never a load balancer or a VIP.

    The memory store is one process and is for a trial only. See Stores.

    etcd disk¶

    WAL fsync p99 under 10 ms

    etcd needs a disk with sustained WAL fsync p99 under 10 ms, which is etcd's own guidance. Datacenter NVMe and cloud SSD volumes qualify. A consumer QLC drive does not.

    A drive that stalls pushes port49's store calls past the 500 ms call timeout in bursts, and a store call that times out answers a token login store_unavailable. The etcd_disk_slow alert fires when the WAL fsync p99 or the backend commit p99 stays over its bound for 10 minutes. It reads etcd's own metrics, so scrape etcd.

    On a GCE pd-ssd (400 GB, declared by the operator) the load run passed every cell with a WAL fsync mean of 0.72 to 0.86 ms. A consumer QLC drive failed it. The figures and the gates are in load-beehive-gates.md, and the other load-* files in tests/lab/results/ are the rest of the run.

    port49d's accounting spool directory needs the same, under 10 ms. That figure is port49's alert threshold, not etcd's. port49d acknowledges each accounting record only after an fsync, so a slow disk caps accounting latency. The port49_accounting_spool_disk_slow alert watches it.

    broker.state_dir¶

    broker.state_dir holds the revocation marks, the witness and the issuing CA. It must survive a restart, so put it on a volume that is kept and back it up with the host. Each broker has its own.

    Clocks¶

    Every broker host and every port49d host keeps its clock on NTP. In Kubernetes these are the nodes' clocks, and in a container the host's. A subject's token_ttl may be 30 seconds, so the hosts must disagree by far less than that.

    The broker writes a token with its expiry, which is its own time of minting plus the TTL. port49d honours the token only before that time on its own clock, and the store's leases only clean up later. On etcd, the two clocks therefore decide a login together.

    • A broker behind. A broker 20 seconds behind turns a 30-second token into a 10-second one. One 30 seconds behind mints tokens that are dead on arrival, and every login with one fails token_not_found, the same as a typo. Nothing else points at the clock.
    • A broker ahead. Each token lasts longer by the skew, until its lease reclaims it: at most lease_grace (5 minutes) plus two minutes past token_ttl. port49d says so (clock: a token was minted ... ahead of this host's clock), counts it in port49_token_minted_ahead_total and raises the port49_token_clock_skew ticket. Skew never admits a token minted for another device or one already spent. It widens an unspent token's window only up to that bound.
    • A session ceiling follows the broker's own auth_time for a certificate login, and for OIDC the provider's, capped at the broker's now. Skew moves it by the skew. A broker behind makes a context look staler (context_stale sooner). One ahead cannot push a refresh time into the future.

    The memory store is one process and has one clock.

    port49 cannot see a broker that is behind, so alert on the hosts' own NTP sync. With node_exporter, alert on node_timex_sync_status == 0 and on abs(node_timex_offset_seconds) > 1.
