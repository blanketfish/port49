# L85: MetalLB in BGP mode as an anycast address for port49d (kind, FRR peer)

Platform: kind v0.29.0, node image kindest/node v1.33.1, on a GitHub-hosted ubuntu-24.04 runner (image
20261026.354.1, 2 vCPU, 8 GB, kernel 7.0.0-1017-azure, Docker 28.0.4). MetalLB chart and app 0.16.1, FRR
10.5.3, address 10.85.85.1. Measured 2026-10-31.

Capture pointer: GitHub Actions run 41155086403, attempt 1, artifact `lab-l85` (`l85-results.md`,
`l85-cells.json`).

## Three runs, stated as they came out

1. Run 41142675001, no `preStop`: (c) PASS, the route left 6.6 s after the pod's deletion; (d) FAIL, one
   login in 42 failed past its retry during the rolling restart. The likely cause is that the listener closes at
   the deletion while the route is still advertised; run 1 did not isolate it.
2. Run 41151306253, the chart's `preStop` of 15 s (6c4fc8d3): (a), (b) and (d) PASS (56/56 logins, 0 retries);
   (c) FAIL on its original bound, the route leaving 21.6 s after the deletion. In that window 29/29
   per-transaction logins passed first time and all 4 single-connection sessions closed cleanly after 20
   logins.
3. This run, 41155086403, the same chart: all four cells PASS on the redefined (c).

Why the bound changed: the first definition timed the withdrawal from the pod's deletion. A pod with a
`preStop` keeps serving and keeps its route for that long on purpose, so the deletion was the wrong clock,
and the measurement it failed was the intended behaviour. (c) now times from the listener closing (read from
the deleted pod's log), still within 10 s, and adds that no per-transaction login fails past one retry in the
window. The deletion-to-withdrawal delay is an observation. The redefinition was made after seeing run 2's
numbers; run 1's design (no `preStop`) is the case it was written to explain, not to excuse.

## Observations in this run

- (a) PASS. A login from the device's own address passed; one from the router's address, which the inventory
  does not name, was reset.
- (b) PASS. FRR's next hops (BGP and forwarding tables) were exactly the two nodes running a ready port49d
  pod, of three workers.
- (c) PASS. The listener closed 15.1s after the deletion, the node left FRR's next hops 6.6s after that
  (21.7s after the deletion), and 33/33 per-transaction logins passed first time. Of the 4 single-connection
  sessions, 2 closed after 20 logins and 2 were still open at the end of the window (after 29 and 31 logins),
  which the judge does not fail.
- (d) PASS. A rolling restart took 48s to settle; 55/55 logins passed first time.
