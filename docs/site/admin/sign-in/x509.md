# Client certificate sign-in

A person signs in with a certificate and key their CA issued. The broker trusts one CA for them
([Identities](../../concepts/identities.md)) and checks with that CA whether a certificate has been revoked.

## Set it up

1. Name the CA. A broker that only takes client certificates sets `broker.client_ca_file`. A broker that also takes
   OIDC names the CA in an `identity:` block, which replaces `client_ca_file`:

    ```yaml
    broker:
      identity:
        humans:
          - {type: x509, trust_anchor_file: /etc/port49/broker/client-ca.pem}
    ```

2. Say how revocation is checked. `certificate_status` is required wherever a client certificate verifier is
   configured, in the broker block and in the `tls_listener` block, so skipping the check is always a choice you wrote
   down. The shipped configs ask each certificate's own OCSP responder. To use CRLs, name each under `crls:`:

    ```yaml
    broker:
      certificate_status:
        crls:
          - name: corp_ca
            url: http://crl.example.com/corp-ca.crl
            issuer_file: /etc/port49/corp-ca.pem
        # ocsp: {use_aia: true}
        refresh_interval: 10m
        on_unavailable: refuse
    ```

3. Restart the broker, then sign in with a test certificate. To check, run:

    ```sh
    port49 login --broker https://<broker>:<port> --broker-ca <broker-ca.pem> --cert <client-cert.pem> --key <client-key.pem>
    ```

    Success prints `port49: logged in to <broker>; the session ends at <time>`. A broker that cannot find a status
    source answers `certificate_status_unavailable` (see below).

!!! warning "Choose CRLs before the first sign-in if your CA has no OCSP responder"
    AD CS and step-ca often have none. The shipped setting refuses every certificate whose CA names no responder, and the
    broker's log says why.

Never set `disabled: true` outside a trial. To check nothing, you write it out, and it takes no other key:

```yaml
broker:
  certificate_status:
    disabled: true
```

port49d says so at every start: ``certificate status checking is disabled (broker.certificate_status.disabled): a certificate revoked at its CA keeps minting until `port49 revoke --cert` ``. With it, only `port49 revoke` ends a certificate.

The device side is on [TLS](../../operate/tls.md#revocation). Every key is in the
[port49d reference](../../reference/config/port49d.md).

## The sources

- A CRL is a `file` or an `http(s)` `url` (exactly one), a unique `name` (lowercase letters, digits and `_`) and an
  `issuer_file`. Lists are fetched or re-read every `refresh_interval` (10 minutes by default, 1 minute to 24 hours).
  A CRL served over https from a private CA needs that CA's root in the broker's trust store, which is the system's or
  a file named by `SSL_CERT_FILE` in the broker's environment (on systemd, `systemctl edit port49-broker`).
- An OCSP block takes a fixed `responder` or `use_aia: true`, and `hash: sha1` for a responder that knows no SHA-256
  CertID.
- Each verified list is kept as `crl-<name>.der` in `broker.state_dir`, so a restart during a publisher outage keeps
  serving it until its next update. Back these up with the rest of the state.

### issuer_file

`issuer_file` is required on every CRL. It is the CA certificate that signs that list, and port49d refuses to start,
naming the source, unless the file:

- holds exactly one PEM block, a certificate, and nothing else;
- is a CA certificate, since only a CA signs a list;
- is the trust anchor (`client_ca_file`, or the x509 trust anchor of a workload verifier), or a certificate that
  anchor signed. A list for an issuer the broker does not already trust would vouch for certificates it never trusted.

A list is then used only if it verifies against that certificate: signed by it, with an issuer name encoded exactly as
the certificate's subject, a matching authority key identifier, not dated in the future and not signed with a weak
algorithm. A delta CRL, a list with a critical extension port49 does not understand, or one scoped in a way its
certificates do not name is refused and counted at `port49_crl_loads_total{outcome="refused"}`. The log says why.

An intermediate CA needs the root's list too. Name the root's CRL (`issuer_file` is your `client_ca_file` anchor), or
every certificate under the intermediate is refused, because nothing else says whether the intermediate itself was
revoked. An OCSP responder the root signs for covers it as well. At start port49d says which CA nothing covers and
never refuses to start over it. Under `on_unavailable: refuse` the line is `certificates issued by <CA> will be refused
...: nothing covers <CA> itself; name the root CA's CRL`. Under `allow` it is `certificates issued by <CA> will be
admitted with their revocation status unchecked (on_unavailable: allow): nothing covers <CA> itself; name the root
CA's CRL`, and those certificates are let in.

## When the status is unknown

`on_unavailable` decides what happens when a certificate's status cannot be established, because a CRL is past its next
update or an OCSP responder is down.

- `refuse` (the default): the broker answers `503 certificate_status_unavailable` with `Retry-After: 60`, reason
  `trust_domain_unresolved`. The agent is told to try again and nothing is minted for that certificate.
- `allow` fails open. The connection is admitted and the broker says so (`admitting a certificate whose revocation
  status cannot be established`). Logins keep working through an outage, and a certificate revoked during the outage
  gets in until the list returns. It is the reviewed escape, not a setting to leave on.

A certificate from an issuer with no CRL or OCSP source follows the same setting. Under `refuse` it is refused until a
source covers it, so retrying does not help: add the CRL. Under `allow`, a certificate from an issuer nothing covers is admitted with its revocation status unchecked.

What the broker refuses and what it marks:

- A certificate a CRL or OCSP lists as revoked, or one under a revoked issuing CA, is refused with `credential_revoked`
  at login and at every mint.
- If the certificate holds a connection, a device session or an unspent token, port49d also marks it as `port49 revoke
  --cert` would, with origin `crl <name>` or `ocsp <responder>`. A revoked issuing CA is refused and not marked.
  [Revoking access](../revocation.md#lifting) says why lifting such a mark does not help.
- Freshness follows the broker's clock, so an NTP step forward can make a fresh list stale. Keep time synchronized on
  every broker host.

Watch `port49_crl_next_update_timestamp_seconds` (an hour before a list passes its next update, at
`time() - port49_crl_next_update_timestamp_seconds > -3600`), `port49_cert_status_unavailable_total` and
`port49_crl_loads_total`. They carry a `component` label of `broker` or `tls_listener` and are in the
[metrics reference](../../reference/metrics.md). The TLS alerts are on the
[alerts reference](../../reference/alerts.md#port49_crl_next_update_passed).
