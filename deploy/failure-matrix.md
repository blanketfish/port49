# Failure matrix

Design §12 as an operator reads it: what the device does, what you see, what you do, and the evidence
that says so. Every row names a subtest of `tests/compose/failmatrix_test.go`
(`TestFailureMatrix/<row>`, run by `make failure-matrix` and the `failmatrix` CI job), an existing test,
or a closed lab row. `TestEveryMatrixRowHasEvidence` fails when a row of design §12 is missing here or has
no evidence.

## At 3am

| You see | What the devices do | Do this | Page |
|---|---|---|---|
| port49d is up and its store is failing (`port49_store_health_failures` rising) | port49d answers a token login `ERROR` `store_unavailable` and every command is denied, for up to 5.5 s (`close_after` 5 x `interval` 1 s + `call_timeout`), then closes TCP 49. **Cisco IOS XE treats that `ERROR` as final, so break-glass is refused for those 5.5 s.** **Junos under the reference `authentication-order tacplus` (Decision 3) does the same.** After the close, devices find the connection refused and fall to their local method | Wait the 5.5 s, or stop port49d to get there at once. Then fix the store | `port49_store_health_failures_close` |
| SR Linux, same case | Break-glass logs in at once: with `exit-on-reject true` an `ERROR` is not final for the local method (it logged in and ran a command in 2.4 s, L87 a), and once port49d is dark it does the same in 2.3 s (L87 b). A port49 user's token login is refused for the 5.5 s. Measured in `tests/lab/results/L87-nokia_srlinux.md` | Nothing to wait for. Fix the store | `port49_store_health_failures_close` |
| `port49_store_restore_detected_timestamp_seconds` above 0, and the drop line on port49d's stderr (`the session store was restored from a snapshot`) | A token minted before the restore is refused `FAIL` `token_spent` once (port49d compares the token's store revision with the revision it read the restore at); the user mints again and the new token logs in. Break-glass is as without the restore. A restore taken without `--bump-revision` also refuses, for 20 minutes, a token minted within 5 minutes after port49d's read whose revision is at or below the old timeline's; the runbook uses `--bump-revision`, which needs no such band. The one case port49d does not refuse is a non-bumped restore to a second old snapshot that no read tells, within 20 minutes, with a broker clock more than 5 minutes ahead of port49d's (spec decision 56 ruling 1) | Start a broker, whose purge removes what is left, and follow the restore runbook (design §13); there is no need to stop port49d. After a restore that also restored the spool or `broker.state_dir` (Velero, a volume snapshot), the token delete of the runbook's step 3 is required: neither port49d nor a broker can see that restore. Revocations made after the snapshot are lost by any restore: re-issue them | none yet (the ticket `port49_store_restore_detected`) |
| `port49_redemption_restore_unjudged` at 1 on port49d | port49d cannot read `meta/witness`, so it cannot tell whether its store was restored, and refuses every token login with an `ERROR` `store_unavailable` until a read answers. IOS XE and Junos under `tacplus` alone treat that `ERROR` as final, so break-glass is refused too until port49d is stopped (devices then find TCP 49 refused and fall to their local method) | Fix the store, or the daemon role's read of `meta/witness` (the log says which: a role that cannot read it is named). Stop port49d only to let devices fall to break-glass at once | `port49_redemption_restore_unjudged` (page, 2m) |
| After a restore, `port49_broker_mint_gate_closed` at 1 on a broker, `POST /v1/token` answered `503 store_unavailable` with `Retry-After: 5` | A broker that owes the restore's purge mints nothing until the purge is done, so a token minted in that window is never deleted by it. Token logins of tokens already held are unaffected; `port49 token` retries | Wait: the runner's purge line (`every unspent token is purged`) ends it on the runner, and the other replicas open once they find a runner running the keyspace. A broker with no `witness.json` (a new or lost `broker.state_dir`) that has seen no runner write a witness since it started also purges once at its first takeover, so every unspent token is minted again: expected on a cold start of the whole fleet on new `state_dir`s. If the gauge stays at 1 for more than a minute, no runner is purging: check that one holds the keyspace (`port49_store_keyspace_held` summed over the brokers is 1) and read the runner's stderr for `the takeover stopped` or a purge that failed; it is tried again by the next takeover | `port49_broker_mint_gate_closed` (ticket, 1m) |
| TCP 49 refused at once | The device treats port49 as down and uses its local method | Start port49d, or leave it stopped until the store is back | |

| **Store hangs or loses quorum** | A token login is answered `ERROR` `store_unavailable` and every command `FAIL` `store_unavailable` (design §12; spec §5.7: an authorization is never `ERROR`), within the packet deadline, then the listener closes (5.5 s at the defaults), and opens again once the store answers, an attempt that succeeds (they are 1 s apart for the first ten, then back off to every 10 s, each with 20% jitter, so up to 1.2 s after etcd answers in the first ten) and `open_after` health passes (3 s at the defaults), about 9 s after the members resume when the first attempt fails (`store_hangs`' 9.2 s bound); one member dark, measured in CI run 41769703556 (commit 3ec4349f, six attempts, all passed). **A follower dark** refuses no login: 0 of 30 logins refused in every attempt, the slowest 271 ms (`one_member_dark` logs the leader and pauses a follower). The redemption and the context swap are re-sent on another member, and a member whose call just timed out is not chosen for 15 s, so a mint (which is not re-sent: a second copy would write a second token) does not land on it: no mint needed a retry, the slowest took 144 ms. `ExtendReceipt` and `DeleteToken` are not re-sent either. **A raft leader dark** costs a refusal window while the others elect one (`leader_dark`, 6 rounds per attempt, a new leader each time): over 36 rounds the last refused login came at most 8.2 s after the pause, and most under 3 s. One round in about 54 (this run plus the one before it, d61a41dc) refused for 17.5 s, past the bound below, with the redeem denied `store_unavailable` within milliseconds for several seconds and no circuit-open line; its cause is not established and it is under investigation (`port49_store_fast_fail_total` and the matrix's counter dump are there to name it). The context feed and the marks feed each run as two watches on two different members, merged by revision, so a leader or follower dark leaves them following; a watch quiet for 2 s while the store answers is opened again on a member neither is on. The subtest fails if a refusal outlasts the sum of the election timeout, `close_after` x `health.interval`, `call_timeout`, `open_after` x `health.interval`, `open_for` and 2 s of slack (16.5 s at the stack's settings), and logs each round's timeline from the pause: the first failed health check, the listeners' close and reopen from port49d's log, and the first login that passed. A device retries a refused login. Whether a device falls back to its local account during the window is per platform: see L88 cell (c) in spec §15. Whether the breaker's `open_for` can stretch a short election into a longer refusal is a pre-beta question | `port49_store_health_failures >= 5`, `port49_store_circuit_open` (also `port49_store_marks_feed_down`, the same outage) | Restore quorum; the listener returns on its own | `TestFailureMatrix/store_hangs`, `TestFailureMatrix/one_member_dark`, `TestFailureMatrix/leader_dark` |
