    Troubleshooting¶

    Start from what you were told or what you can see. This index has three parts:

    • A person tells you…: a report of a login that failed.
    • An alert fired: a family of alerts, with what to do and what not to do.
    • A log line says…: a line in port49's log that you do not recognise.

    Each entry gives You see, It means, Do this and Don't. Two pages go with it: the failure matrix says what each device does when each part of port49 is down, and When it will not start covers a process that refuses to run.

    Two commands answer most first questions. port49 explain --device <device> asks the broker, through your agent, what port49 would do for you on that device. port49 store status (run on a host that reaches the metrics port) prints whether each store answers, and exits 3 when the directory store does not.

    A person tells you…¶

    "I cannot log in to one device"¶

    You see: one person, or everyone, fails on one device; the same logins work on other devices.

    It means: the fault is in what port49 knows about that device or that person's grant on it, not in port49 as a whole. The decision log record for the attempt carries a reason:

    • no_grants_matched: no grant of theirs selects the device.
    • token_not_found or token_spent: the token expired, was already used, or was minted for another device. See tokens, receipts and contexts.
    • An unvalidated record, and a connection the device sees reset with no reply: no key validated what the device sent. Its unvalidated.diagnosis says why: no_known_key_matches (a wrong key), matches_retired (a retired key), unadmitted_source (the address is not in the inventory), device_quarantined, admission_refused or transport_mismatch (the device reached the listener it does not speak).

    Do this: have the person run port49 explain --device <device>; it names the grant and the reason. Look the attempt up in the decision log by device.nas; for an unvalidated record, by the source address. For a key fault, give the device the current key (port49 keys show).

    Don't: restart port49d or the broker: a fault confined to one device is not cured by a restart, and a restart discards what the process holds in memory. Don't widen the policy to "see if it works"; the reason names what is wrong.

    "I cannot log in to any device"¶

    You see: logins fail everywhere, or devices ask for a local password. Alerts that may be firing: port49_target_down, port49_target_absent, port49_store_health_failures_close.

    It means: one of four things:

    • port49d is down or unreachable. The devices find TCP 49 refused and fall to their local method.
    • port49d is up and its store is failing. It answers a token login ERROR store_unavailable and every command FAIL store_unavailable, then closes its listeners after 5.5 seconds at the defaults. After the close, devices fall to local.
    • The broker is down. No new token can be minted; a token already held still logs in, and an open session runs on.
    • port49d cannot read meta/witness after a restore (port49_redemption_restore_unjudged). It refuses every token login until a read answers.

    Do this: curl <port49d-host>:9049/readyz and port49 store status; read the first alert that fired; then the matching row of a cut region at 3am or the failure matrix. Use break-glass (the next entry) while you fix it.

    Don't: restart port49d in a loop to "wake it": while the store is down it waits with its listener closed and comes back by itself when the store answers. Don't change the devices' AAA configuration to get around the outage; the devices already fall to local.

    "Break-glass does not work"¶

    You see: a local account is refused on a device while port49 is failing.

    It means: it depends on the platform, and on how long the device has been failing.

    • An ERROR is final on some platforms. While port49d answers ERROR (its store is failing):

      • Cisco IOS XE refuses the local account (the IOS XE note).
      • So does Junos under the reference authentication-order tacplus (vJunos-router, vSRX).
      • Cisco IOS XR over TLS does the same (the note).
      • Nokia SR Linux logs the local account in at once (the note).

      On the platforms that refuse it, the local account works again once port49d closes its listener (about 5.5 seconds at the defaults) or is stopped. The device pages say what each other platform does: IOS XE, IOS XR, Junos.

    • A device that failed over keeps answering locally for a while. After its servers failed, the device itself answers for a window and sends port49 nothing: a token login is refused during it, and break-glass works. Measured (cells f2 and f3 for IOS XE, cells a and f for XR, of the closed reference-configuration row): IOS XE 30 to 39 seconds over TCP (the IOS XE note), and about a minute over TLS on one release, measured once; IOS XR 24 to 46 seconds (the XR note). XR also accepts any local account's password in that window, including one port49 denies. Expect the window after port49d returns from a stop. deploy/device-aaa/README.md has the cells and the other notes.

    Unmeasured

    Whether an authentication ERROR is final on Cisco IOS XR over plain TCP is not measured (L93, open). Plan as if break-glass is refused until port49d closes its listener, and keep a console path.

    Do this: to get to break-glass at once, stop port49d: the devices then find TCP 49 refused and use their local method. Otherwise wait for the listener to close, or for the device's window to end. Keep a console or second path for IOS XE and XR. Test break-glass on each platform before you need it.

    Don't: don't try to shorten a device's window with deadtime on IOS XE: it does not accept the command. Don't leave local accounts on XR beyond break-glass ones, because of the window above.

    "My session ended early"¶

    You see: a person's session ended before they expected: port49 ssh exits 255, the agent says the session ended, or a command is denied context_stale or credential_revoked.

    It means: one of the planned ends, which the decision log says apart. The refresh or revoke record just before the denial names it:

    • the session reached max_session_age, or a token ran past receipt_idle_ttl or receipt_max_age (token_spent);
    • an administrator revoked the person (revocation);
    • an OIDC back-channel logout ended it;
    • a policy change withdrew their grant, or the directory no longer maps them;
    • context_stale: no directory refresh verified them for session.staleness_ceiling (4 hours by default). port49_directory_refresh_half_ceiling pages at half of that.

    Do this: read that person's last records in the decision log. For a stale context, restore the directory servers; the person signs in again once a refresh passes.

    Don't: lift a revocation before reading the revoke record: it says who made it and why. Don't set session.staleness_ceiling to 0s to quiet context_stale; that removes the bound on how old a verification may be.

    "It says the principal was revoked at …"¶

    You see: port49 token or port49 ssh prints this principal was revoked at <time>; nothing is minted for it until an operator lifts the revocation (port49 revoke --lift). A certificate revoked at its CA reads this certificate was revoked at its CA (<origin>) and marked at <time>; nothing is minted for it; request a new certificate.

    It means: a standing revocation mark is in the store, and the person is refused credential_revoked at every mint. The mark does not expire.

    Do this: port49 revoke --list shows the marks in force. If the revocation was a mistake, lift it: port49 revoke --principal <principal> --lift. A certificate the CA revoked is not fixed by a lift: the person needs a new certificate.

    Don't: undo a revocation by restoring the store from a snapshot: a restore is the one routine operation that can admit a revoked person. Don't lift a mark you did not read first.

    An alert fired¶

    Every alert's runbook_url opens its section of the alerts reference, which gives the expression and why the threshold is what it is. The families below follow the rule file. A page alert means port49 is refusing devices or losing records now; a ticket costs nothing yet.

    Scrape and process¶

    Alerts: page port49_target_down (a target does not answer its scrape) and port49_target_absent (no port49d target exists, so every device has lost port49 and falls to local); ticket port49_broker_target_absent.

    Do this: check the process (systemctl status, docker compose ps, kubectl get pods) and curl :9049/readyz. Check that the Prometheus job names are port49d and port49-broker.

    Don't: read port49_target_absent as "port49d is down" before you check the scrape config: a wrong job name fires it too. See monitoring.

    Connections¶

    Alerts: page port49_global_cap_shedding; ticket port49_connections_near_cap.

    Do this: raise limits.max_connections or add replicas. The shed devices were reset on purpose and tried their next server.

    Don't: restart port49d to clear it. A restart resets every open connection and does not change the cap. See monitoring.

    Keys and the issuing CA¶

    Alerts: page port49_issuing_ca_missing and port49_key_retirement_within_1d; ticket port49_key_secret_files_changed, port49_issuing_ca_expiring, port49_key_retirement_within_7d.

    Do this: for a retirement, read the decision log for the devices still on the key and move them to the current one before retire_after. For a missing CA, copy it from a broker that holds it. See key rotation.

    Don't: run init-issuer on a second broker (it makes a different CA), and don't run forget-issuer (every certificate the old CA issued stops working). Don't take a port49_key_version_devices gauge that fell after a restart as proof the devices moved: a restart blanks it. Don't restart to "apply" a changed key file until it holds the key you intend; the process keeps the key it holds until the next start.

    Policy, mint and admin calls¶

    Alerts: page port49_admin_touch_refused (an admin call was refused its hardware-key touch proof: a stolen token or a broken key) and port49_per_login_mfa_refused (a run of MFA proofs did not verify); ticket port49_policy_fail_closed_compositions, port49_policy_file_refused, port49_policy_refresh_pending, port49_policy_bundle_mismatch, port49_broker_mint_gate_closed, port49_devices_generic_platform, port49_context_refreshes_failed.

    Do this: for the two pages, read the admin_touch or per_login_mfa records for the principal and ask the person; revoke if it was not them. For a policy ticket, port49 policy test names the compositions that fail closed, and a refused policy file means an older policy is still serving: fix the file or its directory's mode.

    Don't: write off a touch refusal as a flaky key. Don't read port49_policy_file_refused as "the change took effect".

    Stores¶

    Alerts: page port49_store_directory_unreachable, port49_store_marks_feed_down, port49_store_calls_refused, port49_crypt_store_key_unknown and port49_store_health_failures_close; ticket port49_store_directory_stale, port49_store_logouts_pending, port49_store_feed_down, port49_store_feed_legs_colocated, port49_store_circuit_open, port49_contexts_reindexed, port49_contexts_reindex_failures, port49_store_keyspace_unheld, port49_store_client_cert_expiring, port49_crypt_token_open_failures, port49_directory_pins_unopenable, port49_store_feed_rescans, port49_ledger_pending, port49_ledger_behind, port49_store_health_check_failing, port49_context_cache_stale.

    Do this: run port49 store status, then follow a cut region at 3am. While a region cannot read the directory, revocations made elsewhere do not reach it: to cut someone off there now, remove their grants from the policy file that region loads, or stop its port49d. For port49_store_health_failures_close the listeners reopen by themselves once the store answers. For port49_crypt_store_key_unknown, fix the key window (a mis-set PORT49_ENC_V1) before anything else.

    Don't: restart port49d to clear a store alert: a process that restarts in a cut serves from a snapshot only up to session.staleness_ceiling. Don't alert on or probe /readyz for store health; it does not report it. Don't point a new key at an old store: give a new key a new id.

    TLS (RFC 9887)¶

    Alerts: page port49_tls_server_certificate_expired; ticket port49_tls_handshake_failures, port49_tls_device_certificate_renewal_late, port49_tls_server_certificate_renewal_late, port49_tls_cert_status_unavailable, port49_crl_next_update_passed, port49_inventory_transport_changed.

    Do this: replace the listener's certificate; the outcome label of the handshake alert says why a handshake failed. See TLS.

    Don't: switch devices to plain TCP as a workaround. A device speaks one transport, and the other listener declines it.

    Identity providers and restores¶

    Alerts: page port49_redemption_restore_unjudged; ticket port49_store_restore_detected, port49_oidc_keys_stale, port49_oidc_logouts_failing, port49_oidc_tokens_unavailable, port49_workload_certificates_renewal_late.

    Do this: for port49_redemption_restore_unjudged, fix the store or the daemon role's read of meta/witness; the log line carries the store's own error. Stop port49d only to let devices fall to break-glass at once. For a detected restore, follow backup and restore.

    Don't: expect a restart to clear the unjudged page: it clears when a read of meta/witness answers. Don't skip re-issuing the revocations a restore lost.

    Directory and password sign-in¶

    Alerts: page port49_directory_refresh_half_ceiling, port49_directory_enrollment_store_lost and port49_sign_ins_enrollment_store_lost; ticket port49_directory_refresh_stale, port49_directory_lookups_ambiguous, port49_directory_server_down, port49_directory_group_names_ambiguous, port49_sign_ins_throttled.

    Do this: for the refresh page, restore the directory servers before the ceiling: at it, every live session is denied context_stale. For a lost enrollment store, restore the file or run port49 directory import from the export. When the broker's line names a check instead (an owner, a mode, a link, a size), fix what it names. For example, after a restore left /var/lib/port49/enrollments.json owned by uid 1000, it reads the file /var/lib/port49/enrollments.json: owner uid 1000, wants uid 999 or root - chown it. The line names each path by its last 32 bytes, so a deeper path starts with …. The file is enrollments.json in broker.state_dir, or beside broker.revocations_file when broker.state_dir is unset. See backup and restore.

    A directory server that stops answering without closing its connection (a firewall or NAT that dropped its state, a VIP whose server was replaced) costs one query its broker.directory.query_timeout: when nothing at all was read on the connection by then, the broker closes it and the next query dials a new one. A brief burst of slow mints with port49_directory_server_up still 1 is that; port49_directory_queries_total{outcome="fault"} counts each one. The health check dials a connection of its own and does not clear faults that queries counted; only a query that answers does. A server that keeps timing out therefore opens its breaker after circuit_breaker.failure_threshold faults in a row, even at a site with few sign-ins. Each such query dials and binds a new connection (a successful bind, which counts nothing toward the service account's lockout), so a server in this state costs a bind and a full query_timeout per query until its breaker opens.

    Don't: run init-enrollments to fix a lost store on an install that had enrolled people: it makes an empty store, and everyone enrolls again. Restore the export instead. Don't use the escapes in the alert (session.staleness_ceiling: 0s, or stopping port49d) as a routine fix; each gives up a bound.

    Accounting and the decision log¶

    Alerts: page port49_accounting_spool_drops, port49_decision_log_write_failures and port49_accounting_foreign; ticket port49_accounting_spool_disk_slow.

    Do this: for write failures, fix the sink (a full disk, a mode); decisions are not being recorded until you do. The stage label of the spool alert says where records were discarded. For foreign, look for break-glass and console logins: a local login during an outage is expected.

    Don't: ignore the write-failure page: a decision that is not recorded cannot be reconstructed. Don't treat foreign as a port49 bug before you check the device's local accounts. See monitoring.

    Inventory¶

    Alerts, all ticket: port49_inventory_sync_failing, port49_inventory_sync_refused, port49_inventory_bundle_refused, port49_inventory_mirror_behind, port49_inventory_persist_failed, port49_inventory_snapshot_stale, port49_inventory_policy_mismatch, port49_inventory_privilege_changes.

    Do this: read the inventory service's log. The fleet keeps authenticating on the snapshot in force, and nothing fails closed. For port49_inventory_privilege_changes, the inventory_change record names the device: review it.

    Don't: restart port49d to refresh the inventory; the bundle in force keeps serving until a good one arrives.

    etcd¶

    Alerts: page etcd_no_leader; ticket etcd_db_nearly_full, etcd_disk_slow, etcd_leader_changes.

    Do this: bring back a quorum. A dark member costs up to 12 seconds; a lost quorum stops the store, and port49d answers store_unavailable and then closes its listeners. For a full database, compact and defragment as etcd's documentation describes, before it reaches its quota. For a slow disk, see monitoring.

    Don't: restart every member at once. Don't leave etcd on a disk that cannot hold a WAL fsync p99 under 10 ms.

    A log line says…¶

    Each You see is the line as port49 writes it, with <…> for the part that varies.

    Cached directory membership¶

    You see: serving <principal>'s cached directory membership (its pin as this region last read it)

    It means: the whole line begins directory store: not answering: and ends a forget made elsewhere reaches this region when the directory answers. This region cannot reach the directory store, and port49 answered for that principal from the pin it last read. A forget made in another region has not arrived here. It is said once per principal per 10 minutes. The table that remembers who was told holds 65,536 principals; past that it drops the oldest, so on a larger fleet the line can repeat for a principal.

    Do this: restore the link to the directory store (port49 store status; the alert port49_store_directory_unreachable). When it heals, the forget reaches the region by itself. See stores.

    Don't: run port49 directory forget in the cut region: it is refused there and changes nothing. Run it from a region that reaches the directory. Don't size the outage by counting these lines.

    The logout queue is full¶

    You see: the logout queue is full; a pass over the whole logout set follows

    It means: the broker's runner received more back-channel logouts from another region than its queue holds. None is lost: each is in the set the runner follows, and a pass over the whole set reaches them. The line is said once per pass owed; port49_broker_logouts_queue_full_total counts each logout that found the queue full.

    Do this: nothing at once. If the counter keeps rising, the runner is behind: look for a store alert.

    Don't: restart the broker to empty the queue; the pass is what delivers the logouts.

    The session store was restored from a snapshot¶

    You see: the session store was restored from a snapshot

    It means: port49d read meta/witness and found that the store went back to an earlier moment. Tokens minted before the restore are refused token_spent once and the users mint again. Revocations and marks made after the snapshot are lost. The full line names the floor revision and says to follow the restore runbook.

    Do this: follow backup and restore, from the revocations step. Users run port49 token again.

    Don't: restart port49d to clear the line, or lift anything, before the runbook says. Don't assume a revoked person is still revoked until the revocations are re-issued.

    A token was minted ahead of this host's clock¶

    You see: clock: a token was minted <N> ahead of this host's clock, at most once every ten minutes, and port49_token_minted_ahead_total rising; the ticket port49_token_clock_skew fires on it.

    It means: the broker that minted the token has a clock N ahead of this port49d's. A token's expiry is the broker's clock plus the TTL, read on port49d's clock, so every token from that broker lives N longer here, until its lease reclaims it: at most lease_grace (5 minutes) plus two minutes past token_ttl. The other direction does not show: a broker N behind shortens each token by N, and past a token_ttl its tokens are dead on arrival, so every login with one fails token_not_found with no other sign. If logins from one broker fail token_not_found right after the token was minted, compare the clocks first.

    Do this: put every broker host and every port49d host on NTP (chronyc tracking, or timedatectl on the host) and confirm they agree to well under the smallest token_ttl. Alert on the hosts' own NTP sync, since port49 cannot see a broker that is behind: with node_exporter, node_timex_sync_status == 0 and abs(node_timex_offset_seconds) > 1.

    Don't: lengthen token_ttl to cover the skew; it widens replay for an unspent token and leaves the clocks wrong for everything else that reads them.

    Shedding¶

    You see: shedding: all <N> TACACS+ connection slots (limits.max_connections) are in use

    It means: port49d's global connection cap is full and each new connection is reset at accept, so the device tries its next server. The line is said when shedding begins, then once a minute as still shedding: <N> connections reset at Accept in the last minute; <N> of <N> slots in use.

    Do this: raise limits.max_connections or add replicas. See monitoring.

    Don't: treat it as an outage: devices are being sent to their next server on purpose.

    A directory outage past 24 hours¶

    You see: no_grants_matched (or identity_unmapped) for people who held sessions, right after a broker restart or a policy reload, during or just after a long directory outage.

    It means: a person's directory membership is stored with a lease of 24 hours from its last verification (broker.directory.grace cannot exceed 24 hours). During an outage nothing refreshes it. Past the lease, a broker restart or any policy reload finds no membership and revokes every directory session, whatever session.staleness_ceiling is, including 0s. It fails closed: nobody keeps access they cannot be shown to hold. Before 24 hours, sessions continue until the ceiling.

    Do this: bring the directory back before 24 hours. When it is back, the people sign in again.

    Don't: restart a broker or edit the policy during a directory outage that is approaching 24 hours. Raising staleness_ceiling does not help.

    Revocations file failed¶

    You see: port49_revocations_file_failed at 1.

    It means: the last write of broker.revocations_file failed (a hung or full disk or mount). The revocation itself stands in the store and in force, but the file is behind, so a restart would forget a mark the file does not hold. port49 revoke exits 3 for this.

    Do this: fix the disk or mount the file lives on, then run the revoke again (port49 revoke exits 3 and says to). Only a revoke or a lift writes the file, so after a write error, such as a full disk, nothing retries on its own and the gauge stays at 1 until you do. The revoke lists every mark in force, rewrites the file with all of them, and the gauge falls to 0. After a hung mount returns, the write that was in flight finishes the catch-up itself, but run the revoke anyway if the gauge has not fallen.

    Don't: restart the broker or port49d while it is at 1.
