    Failure matrix¶

    For each part of port49 that can fail, this matrix gives:

    • what the device does
    • what you see
    • what to do
    • the test or lab note that shows it

    Start with "At 3am", the table of the failures that cost a login. If you begin from a report, an alert or a log line instead, use the 3am index; it links here for the device behaviour.

    What a device does with an ERROR differs by platform. Some fall to their local method at once. Some treat the ERROR as final and refuse break-glass until port49d closes its listener. The rows say which. A platform a row does not name is unmeasured, not safe.

    Every row names its evidence. A row that cites a lab note is measured behaviour. A row that says a case is open or unmeasured means the behaviour there is unknown.

    Each way port49 can fail, as an operator reads it: what the device does, what you see, what you do, and the evidence that says so. Every row names a subtest of tests/compose/failmatrix_test.go (TestFailureMatrix/<row>, run by make failure-matrix and the failmatrix CI job), an existing test, or a closed lab row. TestEveryMatrixRowHasEvidence fails when a row of the design's failure table is missing here or has no evidence.

    At 3am¶

    You see What the devices do Do this Page
    port49d is up and its store is failing (port49_store_health_failures rising) port49d answers a token login ERROR store_unavailable and every command is denied, for up to 5.5 s (close_after 5 x interval 1 s + call_timeout), then closes TCP 49. Cisco IOS XE treats that ERROR as final, so break-glass is refused for those 5.5 s. Junos under the reference authentication-order tacplus (Decision 3) does the same. Cisco IOS XR over TLS does the same (L73, L73-cisco_xrd.md); over TCP it is unmeasured (L93, open). After the close, devices find the connection refused and fall to their local method Wait the 5.5 s, or stop port49d to get there at once. Then fix the store port49_store_health_failures_close
    SR Linux, same case Break-glass logs in at once: with exit-on-reject true an ERROR is not final for the local method (it logged in and ran a command in 2.4 s, L87 a), and once port49d is dark it does the same in 2.3 s (L87 b). A port49 user's token login is refused for the 5.5 s. Measured in tests/lab/results/L87-nokia_srlinux.md Nothing to wait for. Fix the store port49_store_health_failures_close
    port49_store_restore_detected_timestamp_seconds above 0, and the drop line on port49d's stderr (the session store was restored from a snapshot) A token minted before the restore is refused FAIL token_spent once (port49d compares the token's store revision with the revision it read the restore at); the user mints again and the new token logs in. Break-glass is as without the restore. A restore taken without --bump-revision also refuses, for 20 minutes, a token minted within 5 minutes after port49d's read whose revision is at or below the old timeline's; the runbook's own --bump-revision restore needs no such band, but a Velero restore cannot use it, so after one port49d usually arms the band and every token minted in the 5 minutes after its read is refused token_spent (users mint a new token after it: one minted inside the window stays refused until the band ends, 20 minutes after the read; break-glass is unaffected). port49d does not refuse a token only when all of these hold: a non-bumped restore; a second old-timeline restore that no read tells, within 20 minutes; a broker clock more than 5 minutes ahead of port49d's; and a token redeemed between the second snapshot and the first restore Start a broker, whose purge removes what is left, and follow the restore runbook (Restoring etcd from a snapshot); there is no need to stop port49d. After a restore that also restored the spool or broker.state_dir (Velero, a volume snapshot), the token delete of the runbook's step 3 is required: port49d sees that restore only if its <accounting.spool_path>.restore.json is newer than the etcd copy (usual on a busy store, not on an idle one), and a broker sees it on the same terms (its witness.json is then newer than the restored witness) and purges once more at its first takeover. Revocations made after the snapshot are lost by any restore: re-issue them port49_store_restore_detected (ticket)
    port49_redemption_restore_unjudged at 1 on port49d port49d cannot read meta/witness, so it cannot tell whether its store was restored, and refuses every token login with an ERROR store_unavailable until a read answers. IOS XE and Junos under tacplus alone treat that ERROR as final, so break-glass is refused too until a read answers, until go-dark closes the listener (about 5.5 s) when the store itself is failing, or until port49d is stopped (devices then find TCP 49 refused and fall to their local method) Fix the store, or the daemon role's read of meta/witness (the log line carries the store's own error). Stop port49d only to let devices fall to break-glass at once port49_redemption_restore_unjudged (page, 2m)
    After a restore, port49_broker_mint_gate_closed at 1 on a broker, POST /v1/token answered 503 store_unavailable with Retry-After: 5 A broker that owes the restore's purge mints nothing until the purge is done, so a token minted in that window is never deleted by it. Token logins of tokens already held are unaffected; port49 token retries Wait: the runner's purge line (every unspent token is purged) ends it on the runner, and the other replicas open once they find a runner running the keyspace. A broker with no witness.json (a new or lost broker.state_dir) that has seen no runner write a witness since it started also purges once at its first takeover, so every unspent token is minted again: expected on a cold start of the whole fleet on new state_dirs. If the gauge stays at 1 for more than a minute, no runner is purging: check that one holds the keyspace (port49_store_keyspace_held summed over the brokers is 1) and read the runner's stderr for the takeover stopped or a purge that failed; it is tried again by the next takeover port49_broker_mint_gate_closed (ticket, 1m)
    TCP 49 refused at once The device treats port49 as down and uses its local method Start port49d, or leave it stopped until the store is back
