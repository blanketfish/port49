    Roles and grants¶

    Three blocks of the policy file decide who gets what: a role is what may be done (command rules, a privilege level, a session length and an optional per-login proof), a subject is who, and a grant gives a subject some roles in a scope. Every key is in the policy reference. Policy shows how the pieces fit.

    Roles¶

    roles:
  - name: read_only
    privilege: readonly
    permit: ["show **", "terminal length *"]
    max_session_age: 8h
  - name: bgp_operator
    privilege: operator
    permit: ["show bgp **", "clear bgp neighbor **"]
    deny: ["clear bgp all"]
    per_login_mfa: {accept: [hardware_key_touch]}
    • privilege names a level from privilege_levels:. readonly, operator and admin always exist and keep that order. You may add levels with a parent:, the level a platform renders the new one as where it has no rendering.
    • baseline, reach_deny and rpc_entry are port49's own rule names, so a role called any of them is refused at load.
    • permit and deny follow the rules on Policy. attributes: adds attribute strings to the reply, and a grant may add its own.

    max_session_age¶

    max_session_age is how long a person's session on a device may last: at least 1m, or never. The ceiling is the least across the roles that compose on that device. A role that says nothing takes session.max_session_age_default (12 hours unless you set it in port49d.yaml), and if every role says never, so does the session. port49 explain prints one of these lines:

    max_session_age 8h0m0s (the least of its roles')
max_session_age the lesser of 8h0m0s and session.max_session_age_default
max_session_age session.max_session_age_default (no role names a finite one)
max_session_age never (every role says never)

    They mean: every role names a duration; one names none; the default decides; no ceiling. Shortening a role reaches live sessions at the next policy refresh. Lengthening it, or never, reaches a person's sessions only when they run port49 refresh or log in again. port49 policy diff prints a session ceiling: line for the change.

    Subjects¶

    A subject is exactly one of three things:

    Form Who it names Needs
    group: a directory group of people, by name or DN broker.directory (The directory)
    machine_group: a group of workloads, by name or DN a workload verifier whose directory maps the workload to an entry
    identity: literal principals: user:<email>, a SPIFFE ID, system:serviceaccount:<namespace>:<name>, or app:<client_id> nothing 
    subjects:
  - name: netops
    group: "cn=netops,ou=groups,dc=example,dc=com"
    token_ttl: 300s
  - name: ci_runners
    machine_group: "cn=ci-runners,ou=groups,dc=example,dc=com"
    token_ttl: 120s
  - name: svc_backup
    identity: ["system:serviceaccount:netops:backup"]
    username: svc-config-backup
    token_ttl: 60s

    A subject with both group: and machine_group:, or either with identity:, is refused. A subject is one trust domain: a person never matches a machine_group: subject, a workload never matches a group: subject, and attached_roles.machines reaches the machine domain alone. token_ttl (30 to 900 seconds) is required on any subject a grant names.

    machine_group:¶

    A machine_group: names a directory group whose members are workloads. The broker resolves a workload through broker.identity.machines[].directory. With no mapping the subject mints for no one, and port49d's start says so. It declares no username:, and one that does is refused, because one name for many workloads would put them all in one device login. Members take the username their own entry gives, or the one an identity: subject listing the same SPIFFE ID declares. See Workloads.

    The device sees a username, not a principal. The directory supplies a person's, so a group: subject, and an identity: subject for a user: principal, declare none when broker.directory is on. A literal machine identity declares one (username: svc-config-backup above): lowercase printable ASCII, never remote, a p49- name or a platform's system user. A subject whose identities are all SPIFFE IDs may leave it out where the directory mapping supplies one, and an offline port49 policy test reports workload_no_username.

    api: rights¶

    A principal may always mint for itself, end its own sessions (port49 revoke --mine) and explain its own decisions. Acting on other people is a right the subject declares:

    subjects:
  - name: netops_oncall
    group: "cn=netops-oncall,ou=groups,dc=example,dc=com"
    token_ttl: 300s
    api: [revoke_others, explain_others]

    The policy reference lists every right. revoke_others and lift_revocations revoke and lift, and both list the revocations. explain_others explains another person. forget_principals, reset_second_factor, unlock_sign_in and import_enrollments manage directory pins, second factors, locked sign-ins and the enrollment store. Everything but explain_others and the list needs a press of the person's security key, bound to that one call, so an exposed certificate or agent session alone authorizes nothing admin-level. sync, keys_show and replay are reserved and grant nothing. A broker with no attestation roots refuses every admin call (Admin access).

    CI treats an api: list as a grant of authority over other people's sessions. port49 policy test prints a report: api_rights line per subject, and port49 policy diff fails on a subject that gains a right until a session test asserts the whole list (Tests):

    tests:
  - {subject: netops_oncall, device: leaf1, api: [revoke_others, explain_others]}

    A lost right passes. A principal moved to a subject with more rights needs the assertion in a test naming the principal.

    Grants¶

    grants:
  - {subject: netops, scope: dc_fabric, roles: [read_only]}
  - {subject: netops_oncall, scope: dc_fabric, roles: [bgp_operator], max_privilege: admin, expires: "2026-12-01"}

    A grant names a declared subject and scope and at least one role, each listed once. Two grants with the same subject, scope and roles are refused. expires is a date (the start of that day, UTC) or an RFC 3339 time after which the grant stops matching, and policy diff and policy test read the policy at every later expiry as well as now. max_privilege is a ceiling on the level a token from the grant may carry, which is how far enable may take the session. The subject's enable is none (the default), auto or token. With none, nothing escalates to it.

    A grant on an on_missing: include scope is refused, except on a scope with no match key or pinned by name alone (the include-scope refusal). When one person matches several subjects, the grants compose and reordering them never changes a decision: roles are the union, the level is the highest of the roles, the max_privilege ceiling is the highest across the grants, and max_session_age is the least.

    per_login_mfa¶

    A policy may ask for a second factor at each login to the devices it marks. This is a proof at the device login, not the daily sign-in. Write per_login_mfa: on defaults:, a role or a scope:

    defaults:
  per_login_mfa: {accept: [hardware_key_touch, idp_mfa], reuse_for: 15m}
scopes:
  - name: core
    match: {tier: core}
    on_missing: include             # the requirement holds on a device the inventory cannot describe
    security_log: true
    per_login_mfa: {accept: [hardware_key_touch], reuse_for: 5m}
  - name: core_access
    match: {tier: core}
    on_missing: exclude             # the grant is earned by data

    The scope that carries the requirement is include, so a device whose tier is missing is still held to it. A grant on an include scope is refused, so the grant goes on core_access, a second scope with the same match. port49 explain on core1 prints per_login_mfa accept hardware_key_touch (defaults ∩ scope core); reuse_for 5m (scope core); applies to humans. Do not put the requirement on an on_missing: exclude scope to be able to grant on it: port49 policy test reports per_login_mfa_on_missing_exclude, because a sync that drops tier lifts the proof from the device.

    accept is hardware_key_touch (a touch on the token holding the session key), idp_mfa (a fresh identity-provider sign-in showing a second factor) or both, and reuse_for runs from 0m to 8h (15m by default). Leaving the block out means no requirement; there is no none. The strictest level wins: on a device the requirement is the intersection of the accept lists of every level that applies, with the least reuse_for, and an empty intersection is a load error. A scope that selects by an attribute other than name must declare on_missing and security_log: true. A machine principal is exempt unless a level sets applies_to_machines: true, and then proves with hardware_key_touch alone. An unmet requirement answers deny with per_login_mfa_required, or per_login_mfa_unavailable when no method this broker can verify satisfies it. It gates logins and refreshes, never a live session: adding one revokes nothing.

    port49 policy diff fails, as per_login_mfa_weakened, on a weakening that no session test asserts. The test must be new or changed against the old file. Widening the scope above to accept: [hardware_key_touch, idp_mfa] prints this and exits 1:

    per_login_mfa_weakened: netops on core1: accept [hardware_key_touch] reuse_for 5m (defaults ∩ scope core) -> accept [hardware_key_touch, idp_mfa] reuse_for 5m (defaults ∩ scope core); no session test asserts it: add {subject: netops, device: core1, per_login_mfa: {accept: [hardware_key_touch, idp_mfa], reuse_for: 5m}}

    Reading why a grant applied¶

    port49 explain --policy policy.yaml --inventory inventory.yaml --subject netops --device leaf1 shows the grants that matched, the roles, the level, the ceiling, the session age and the per_login_mfa requirement for one device (Reviewing a change). Signed in, port49 identity prints whom the broker resolves you to (subject, grants, api: rights), and port49 explain --device NAME asks the broker from the policy in force. port49 access review --policy FILE --inventory FILE --subject NAME prints one subject's grant table.

    To offboard a machine subject or a literal identity, run port49 grants remove --subject NAME. It prints the policy without the subject's grants and its block, --write replaces the file, and --pr opens the pull request that carries the removal. The result must load against the inventory before anything is written. People in a directory group are offboarded in the directory.
