    Alerts¶

    port49.rules.yaml holds one Prometheus alerting rule for each alarm port49 defines. Load it in one of two ways:

    • into Prometheus, with rule_files:
    • through the Helm chart, with prometheusRule.enabled: true. The chart renders the same file as a Prometheus Operator PrometheusRule. deploy/helm/port49/files/port49.rules.yaml is a byte copy, because Helm cannot read outside the chart, and tests/alerts fails if the two differ.

    make alerts-check runs promtool check rules and promtool test rules on it. go test ./tests/alerts/ checks that every metric an expression names is one port49 registers.

    There are two severities; route on the severity label.

    • page: a fault that is refusing devices or losing records now.
    • ticket: one that costs nothing yet, or that a person fixes in working hours.

    The job names are the rules' parameter. Three rules read Prometheus's own up series for job="port49d" and job="port49-broker": port49_target_down, port49_target_absent and port49_broker_target_absent. The chart's PodMonitors (rendered with prometheusRule.enabled) set those names. If you scrape another way, give the jobs those names or edit the rules. Without them, port49_target_absent and port49_broker_target_absent fire, which is the point of them.

    The absent rules are fleet-wide: absent() stays quiet while any scrape of that job exists. So use one Prometheus per port49 fleet, or add a namespace matcher to the two expressions.

    No expression names a device, and none groups by one. The series carry no device identity, and the decision log answers "which devices". The labels used are bounded: instance, key_ref, version, server, issuer, outcome, feed, pool, component.

    Each alert below carries a runbook_url annotation that points at its section here. The metrics each rule reads are in the metrics reference. An alert that has just fired: start at the 3am index, which gives a short do and don't for each family of alerts.

    Thresholds and defaults¶

    Each row pairs what an alert covers with the rule that answers it.

    A threshold that follows a setting assumes that setting's default. The defaults are close_after 5, broker.refresh_interval 5m, jwks_max_age 24h, expiry_warning_fraction 0.33 and staleness_ceiling 4h. Edit the rule if you set another. The hold_ttl rules are the exception: they use multiples of the 15s hold_ttl default that hold at any allowed setting (10s to 1m), so they need no edit.

    Edit in the safe direction. A setting lowered below the default can silence its alarm:

    • With close_after: 3, the page on port49_store_health_failures >= 5 never fires.
    • With jwks_max_age: 6h, the 12h keys rule fires after tokens are already refused.

    Recompute each such threshold when you change the setting it follows.

    Notes¶

    The component label. port49_cert_status_checks_total, port49_crl_loads_total, port49_crl_next_update_timestamp_seconds and port49_cert_status_unavailable_total carry component (broker, tls_listener), and the store metrics carry component and pool. A rule that divides or compares two such series one-to-one fails to match across components: aggregate with sum by (...) or match with ignoring(component). Every rule here names its component or needs no match.

    A dark etcd member costs up to 12 s. gRPC raises a keepalive below 10 s to 10 s, plus a 2 s timeout, so a dropped link to one member stalls the marks watch (events resume on another member) and an unhedged call for that long. Reads and the opted-in mark writes are hedged, other writes are not, so no alert can fire faster than that; the for: of the store rules is longer than 12 s for this reason.

    Nothing warns before the listener closes. close_after x interval (5 s at the defaults) is shorter than any scrape interval, so no alert can fire between the first failed health check and the close. port49_store_health_failures_close pages once the listeners are closed: the gauge is held by the process, not the run, so the page stays raised through the whole outage and resolves when the listeners reopen.

    Series that exist only where the thing does. port49_store_directory_reachable, port49_store_directory_confirmed_timestamp_seconds, port49_store_keyspace_held and port49_directory_refresh_last_complete_timestamp_seconds are created by the code that starts what they watch (the etcd store, the keyspace runner, the scheduled refresh), at 0. On the memory backend, the default, they do not exist, so no alert on == 0 or on age has a series to fire on. Where a gauge cannot work that way, a *_configured gauge says whether to expect it: port49_issuing_ca_configured, port49_directory_password_configured.

    Where the rules differ from the statement they answer. - port49_oidc_keys_stale drops the statement's or absent(): every configured issuer's series is created at 0, so an issuer that never fetched already shows time() - 0, and the series is absent only where OIDC is not configured, where absent() would fire forever. - port49_directory_enrollment_store_lost keeps it, as port49_directory_password_configured == 1 unless on(instance) port49_directory_enrollment_store_ok: the gauge is absent until the store first answers, and a broker that never reached it is the case to catch. - port49_issuing_ca_expiring also requires the CA gauge above 0, so the page for a missing CA is not doubled by a ticket for a CA "expiring" in 1970. - port49_store_directory_stale requires its timestamp above 0 (a process that has not yet confirmed is port49_store_directory_unreachable and port49_store_feed_down). - port49_directory_refresh_stale and port49_directory_refresh_half_ceiling take the freshest refresh across all runner-capable replicas, so a replica that lost the hold, whose gauge stops moving, does not alarm; and they require the newest such process to have been up as long as the threshold, so a restart is quiet but a restart in the middle of a directory outage (the gauge reads 0 until a pass completes) still fires. Both wait 15 minutes (for:), which covers a runner failover: the new runner's series sits at 0 until its first pass completes. They read last_over_time(...[1d]), so they keep firing for a day after every broker is gone, when no series is left to read. - port49_inventory_snapshot_stale is the spec's expression with its NaN arm and no gate: a process that never watches the inventory reads 0, and one waiting for its first snapshot reads NaN before its listener opens, which is the case the NaN arm exists for. - port49_store_keyspace_unheld sums over the processes that run a keyspace runner, and fires for a day after the last one disappears (a crashed fleet exports no 0): count(last_over_time(...[1d])) unless count(...). A memory-backend install never had the series, so it never fires. - The key-retirement rules leave out the spec's uptime gate (spec, key rotation: line 5578). That only makes them fire more. A restart blanks port49_key_version_devices (it counts devices whose latest validated connection since start used the version), so a rule that resolves after a restart is not evidence the devices have moved: read the decision log. - enrolled sign-ins outside a rollout are not alerted: "outside a rollout" is yours to define.

    The rules¶

    port49_target_down¶

    Severity: page. For: 2m.

    A port49d or broker target is not answering its scrape: up is 0 for it.

    up{job=~"port49d|port49-broker"} == 0

    Source: A port49d or broker that is down raises no alarm of its own, because every other rule reads series it no longer exports

    Why this threshold: up == 0 for a scrape target of either job; two minutes outlasts a restart's missed scrapes.

    port49_target_absent¶

    Severity: page. For: 5m.

    Prometheus has no up series for port49d: no port49d target exists at all, so every device has lost port49 and falls to local authentication, and no other port49d rule can fire.

    absent(up{job="port49d"})

    Source: A port49d target Prometheus is not scraping at all has no up series, so == 0 never matches

    Why this threshold: absent() of port49d's up. Prometheus's pod discovery keeps a target only while its pod exists, so a StatefulSet with every pod Pending or deleted leaves no series for == 0 to match, and every device has lost port49: a page. Five minutes outlasts a rollout. A mislabelled scrape config shows here too.

    port49_broker_target_absent¶

    Severity: ticket. For: 10m.

    Prometheus has no up series for the broker: no broker target exists at all, so no token can be minted and no other broker rule can fire.

    absent(up{job="port49-broker"})

    Source: The same for the broker: no up series for port49-broker at all

    Why this threshold: absent() of the broker's up: no token can be minted, but held tokens still log in. On the memory-store trial, which runs no separate broker, the broker rule fires; drop it there or silence it.

    port49_global_cap_shedding¶

    Severity: page. For: none.

    port49d reset TACACS+ connections at accept because the global connection cap was full; devices fall to local authentication.

    increase(port49_connections_shed_total{reason="global_cap"}[5m]) > 0

    Source: Connections reset at accept because the global connection cap is full: port49_connections_shed_total{reason="global_cap"} increasing

    Why this threshold: Any shed connection is a device refused, so any increase pages; five minutes smooths one scrape.

    port49_connections_near_cap¶

    Severity: ticket. For: 10m.

    A port49d is holding more than 80% of limits.max_connections open; raise the cap or add replicas before it sheds.

    sum by (instance) (port49_connections{state="open"}) / on(instance) port49_connections_max > 0.8

    Source: A warning before shedding starts: port49_connections near the cap

    Why this threshold: Ten minutes at 80% is a trend, not a burst; the page above covers the cap itself.

    port49_key_secret_files_changed¶

    Severity: ticket. For: 10m.

    A key version's secret file changed or cannot be read; the held secret stays in force until the next start.

    port49_key_secret_files_changed > 0

    Source: A key version's secret file that changed or cannot be read: port49_key_secret_files_changed > 0 for 10m

    Why this threshold: A rotated file is expected for minutes while the rollout restarts; ten minutes is a forgotten restart.

    port49_issuing_ca_missing¶

    Severity: page. For: 5m.

    A broker is configured to issue certificates and has no usable issuing CA, so every OIDC sign-in is refused issuance_unavailable; run port49d init-issuer or restore the CA.

    port49_issuing_ca_not_after_timestamp_seconds == 0 and on(instance) port49_issuing_ca_configured == 1

    Source: A broker set to issue certificates that has no usable issuing CA: port49_issuing_ca_not_after_timestamp_seconds == 0 while broker.identity.issuance is configured

    Why this threshold: A broker started without its CA says so and keeps serving; five minutes outlasts a rollout and nothing else clears it.

    port49_issuing_ca_expiring¶

    Severity: ticket. For: none.

    The issuing CA expires within 90 days; make its successor and distribute the new bundle before sign-ins stop.

    (port49_issuing_ca_not_after_timestamp_seconds - time() < 90 * 86400) and on(instance) (port49_issuing_ca_not_after_timestamp_seconds > 0) and on(instance) port49_issuing_ca_configured == 1

    Source: The issuing CA expires within 90 days

    Why this threshold: Ninety days is a CA rollover's lead time; a CA already at 0 is the page above, not this ticket.

    port49_key_retirement_within_7d¶

    Severity: ticket. For: none.

    A key version with devices still on it passes its retire_after within 7 days; move the devices (the decision log names them) before it does.

    (port49_key_version_devices > 0) and on(key_ref, version) (port49_key_retirement_seconds < 7 * 86400)

    Source: A key version that devices still present nears its retire_after: the first step of an alarm whose severity rises as the date approaches

    Why this threshold: A week is a change window; the series carry no device identity, the log names the devices.

    port49_key_retirement_within_1d¶

    Severity: page. For: none.

    A key version with devices still on it passes its retire_after within a day, or already has; those devices fail authentication once it does.

    (port49_key_version_devices > 0) and on(key_ref, version) (port49_key_retirement_seconds < 86400)

    Source: The same key version within a day of its retire_after, or past it: the alarm's last step

    Why this threshold: A day is the last working shift; a version past retire_after with devices on it is refusing them now.

    port49_admin_touch_refused¶

    Severity: page. For: none.

    An admin call was refused its hardware-key touch proof: a stolen token or a broken key.

    increase(port49_admin_touch_total{outcome="refused"}[10m]) > 0

    Source: An admin call refused its hardware-key touch proof: port49_admin_touch_total{outcome="refused"}

    Why this threshold: Someone holds an admin credential without the touch; no benign cause repeats.

    port49_policy_fail_closed_compositions¶

    Severity: ticket. For: none.

    The policy in force has compositions answered fail-closed: those users are denied all but the allows; port49 policy test names them.

    port49_policy_fail_closed_compositions > 0

    Source: A policy in force that answers some compositions fail-closed: port49_policy_fail_closed_compositions > 0

    Why this threshold: A standing condition of the policy, not an incident; a ticket the author sees.

    port49_policy_file_refused¶

    Severity: ticket. For: 5m.

    The policy file on disk was refused or will not settle; an older policy is serving.

    port49_policy_file_refused == 1

    Source: A policy file on disk that is refused or will not settle: port49_policy_file_refused == 1

    Why this threshold: An edit in progress is refused for seconds; five minutes is a file that stayed wrong.

    port49_policy_refresh_pending¶

    Severity: ticket. For: 2m.

    A policy change has not yet reached every live login context.

    port49_policy_refresh_pending == 1

    Source: A policy swap or grant expiry that has not yet reached every live login context: 1 from the change until its settle pass

    Why this threshold: A pass takes seconds; two minutes is a stuck settle pass.

    port49_policy_bundle_mismatch¶

    Severity: ticket. For: 5m.

    A broker or port49d serves a policy bundle other than the runner's; its tokens or admissions carry another policy until it reloads.

    port49_policy_bundle_matches_runner == 0

    Source: A broker serving a policy bundle other than the runner's: port49_policy_bundle_matches_runner 0 for longer than twice broker.policy_watch_interval

    Why this threshold: Five minutes outlasts twice the watch interval and the settle delay at their defaults; raise it if the intervals are raised.

    port49_broker_mint_gate_closed¶

    Severity: ticket. For: 1m.

    A broker mints nothing: its mint gate has been closed for over a minute; the store does not answer, or no runner judges its bundle.

    port49_broker_mint_gate_closed == 1

    Source: A broker that mints nothing: port49_broker_mint_gate_closed at 1 for a minute

    Why this threshold: The gate is closed from a start until a takeover is seen, so a minute is where a restart ends and a fault begins; a ticket, since other replicas keep minting.

    port49_devices_generic_platform¶

    Severity: ticket. For: 5m.

    Admitted devices are served by the generic adapter: their platform has no adapter, and their commands are judged without its knowledge.

    port49_devices_generic_platform > 0

    Source: Admitted devices served by the generic adapter: port49_devices_generic_platform, naming no device

    Why this threshold: A standing condition of the inventory, not an incident; the start line names the platforms and counts.

    port49_context_refreshes_failed¶

    Severity: ticket. For: none.

    A policy refresh could not carry a login context onto the policy in force: a context revoked for a compile fault, or one that would not open.

    increase(port49_context_refreshes_total{outcome="failed"}[10m]) > 0

    Source: A login context a policy refresh could not carry onto the policy in force: counted failed in port49_context_refreshes_total

    Why this threshold: Each failure is one context left on an older policy or revoked.

    port49_per_login_mfa_refused¶

    Severity: page. For: none.

    A run of per-login MFA proofs did not verify: someone is trying another token or another person's sign-in.

    increase(port49_per_login_mfa_total{outcome="refused"}[10m]) > 2

    Source: A run of per-login MFA proofs that did not verify: per_login_mfa_refused, alerted on its own, because a run of them is someone trying another token or another person's sign-in

    Why this threshold: More than two in ten minutes is a run; a mistyped touch is one.

    port49_store_directory_unreachable¶

    Severity: page. For: 1m.

    This process cannot read the directory store: revocations and password sign-ins from other regions do not arrive.

    port49_store_directory_reachable == 0

    Source: A process that cannot read the directory store: port49_store_directory_reachable == 0 for 1m

    Why this threshold: A cut region keeps logging devices in on stale marks, so one minute is a real partition.

    port49_store_directory_stale¶

    Severity: ticket. For: 1m.

    This process last confirmed its copy of the directory store over a minute ago, whatever the reason; its revocation marks may be stale.

    (time() - port49_store_directory_confirmed_timestamp_seconds > 60) and on(instance) port49_store_directory_confirmed_timestamp_seconds > 0

    Source: A process whose copy of the directory store is unconfirmed for over a minute: time() - port49_store_directory_confirmed_timestamp_seconds > 60

    Why this threshold: The series exists only under etcd, at 0 until the first confirmation, so a 0 is skipped here: port49_store_directory_unreachable and port49_store_feed_down cover a process that never confirmed.

    port49_store_logouts_pending¶

    Severity: ticket. For: 5m.

    Back-channel logouts were accepted while the directory store did not answer and have not reached it.

    port49_store_logouts_pending > 0

    Source: Back-channel logouts accepted but not yet in the directory store: port49_store_logouts_pending > 0 for 5m

    Why this threshold: A cut region holds logouts the others have not seen; five minutes is longer than a blip.

    port49_store_marks_feed_down¶

    Severity: page. For: 1m.

    The marks feed is down: revocation marks written elsewhere do not reach this process.

    port49_store_feed_up{feed="marks"} == 0

    Source: The marks feed down, so revocation marks written elsewhere do not arrive: port49_store_feed_up{feed="marks"} == 0

    Why this threshold: Revocation rides this feed; a minute is four times the 15s hold_ttl default and the longest hold_ttl allows (10s to 1m), so at any setting the feed has been down for a full hold.

    port49_store_feed_down¶

    Severity: ticket. For: 1m.

    A store feed other than marks is down (contexts, or a split broker's pins or logouts): this process misses other replicas' writes, and its directory copy is not confirmed.

    port49_store_feed_up{feed!="marks"} == 0

    Source: A store feed other than marks down: port49_store_feed_up{feed} 0 for longer than hold_ttl

    Why this threshold: A minute is four times the 15s hold_ttl default and the longest hold_ttl allows (10s to 1m). Every feed but marks, which pages, so a feed added later is covered.

    port49_store_feed_legs_colocated¶

    Severity: ticket. For: 10m.

    Both legs of the {{ $labels.feed }} feed's watch are on one etcd member: port49 reaches only one etcd member for this feed, so check the other members; that member going dark stalls the feed.

    port49_store_feed_legs_colocated == 1

    Source: Both legs of a feed's watch on one etcd member: port49_store_feed_legs_colocated at 1 for 10m

    Why this threshold: Both legs of a feed's watch on one member is no redundancy against that member going dark; the move apart is tried from 2 s doubling to 30 s, so ten minutes is a cluster where no other member is reachable for this feed.     Source: An etcd disk too slow, by port49's own threshold: p99 of etcd_disk_wal_fsync_duration_seconds over 10 ms, or of etcd_disk_backend_commit_duration_seconds over 25 ms

    Why this threshold: etcd's own guidance is a WAL fsync p99 under 10 ms; a drive that stalls can push store calls past the 500 ms call timeout in bursts: on a CT1000P3PSSD8 drive the session etcd logged 7974 to 9244 slow applies in cells d and e (tests/lab/results/load-beehive.md), against 1 and 0 in RAM (tests/lab/results/load-beehive-ram.md) and at most 74 on a GCE pd-ssd (operator-declared; a 44, c 74, d 1, e 0) with a WAL fsync mean of 0.72 to 0.86 ms (tests/lab/results/load-p49-gce-n2s16.us-central1-a.c.port49.internal.md). Ten minutes over a 5m rate is a drive, not a compaction.

    etcd_leader_changes¶

    Severity: ticket. For: none.

    etcd changed leader more than 3 times in an hour: a flapping network or a slow disk.

    increase(etcd_server_leader_changes_seen_total[1h]) > 3

    Source: etcd changing leader too often: increase(etcd_server_leader_changes_seen_total[1h]) > 3

    Why this threshold: Occasional changes are rollouts; four in an hour is a fault.
